# Hormes · A day in 90 seconds

Storyboard v5 · October 11 · PRD 1.5

A hypothetical busy student's calendar connects learning, coding, a VC meeting,
Notes-to-Instacart grocery preparation and a quiet break. Show actual product results and saved work.
The calendar is the story thread; each tool drops from the same native notch.

## Prepare the take

Fully quit and relaunch the installed Hormes build. Check microphone permission,
Gemini voice, GPT reasoning and the unlocked desktop. Open Console's Library
view and the supplied Exchange Economies material. Select one simple grocery note
in Apple Notes. Prepare the retailer login, store and delivery address manually
before the take; Hormes does not enter credentials or complete checkout. Use the labeled hypothetical day controls to
advance time; they do not alter the real calendar or prove background anticipation.

Prepare the VC brief in advance. Its PDF deck and retention workbook are labeled
synthetic demo material. The draft uses real attachments. Never show private keys
or claim synthetic cohorts as traction. New sessions preserve previous work;
do not erase genuine work to reset the demo.

The timeline totals ninety seconds. Cut or visibly accelerate long operations;
do not portray an edited task as an instantaneous uncut result. Keep the quote
short and audible:

> This looks interesting. Send me the latest deck and the retention spreadsheet after this, and let’s reconnect next week.

## Direction

Show the notch expansion once at normal speed. During tutoring, keep only the
handwritten board and essential icon controls visible; the tutor's own speech
carries the explanation. Show a real failed attempt before help and retain its
assisted status. Fresh exam endowments change, so answer the current problem.
One checked criterion is progress, not proof of full mastery or delayed recall.

The VC workflow is preparation → request → reviewed work. The deck/spreadsheet
are actual files; the email stays unsent and reconnect timing remains pending.
A typed recap is a valid alternative pre/post shot, explicitly presented as a
recap. The complete microphone path has passed using generated test speech;
a physical voice/notch rehearsal is still needed.

Groceries must show the actual selected note and a separately authored plan. Say,
“Use this grocery list to prepare my Instacart cart.” Film real cart actions,
then the passive review dropping from the notch. Item checks require visible
cart evidence, not merely a successful click. Keep unavailable products and
substitutions explicit. If login, address setup, JavaScript from Apple Events or
a site control blocks progress, show the blocker and resume after the user fixes
it; do not stage a successful cart. Review cart opens the real retailer page.
Checkout is manual and is outside the demo. The calendar is the story cue;
reading arbitrary notes or observing the clock does not authorize purchases.

Close on Console continuity: “Your day moves on. Your work stays with you.”


## Silent lecture rehearsal

Open a labeled demo class, start recording explicitly, and speak a short mock lecture. Teacher captions appear in red. Click Q and type a question while continuing the lecture: the notch grows to show a white written answer, capture stays active, and no AI audio plays. Stop recording only after the next teacher phrase, then inspect the dated transcript and written question in Console. Do not pause capture or switch into the spoken study tutor to ask this question.

The isolated cloud check measured first interim caption at 1.24 seconds, a saved full segment at 11.47 seconds, and a written GPT answer in 4.09 seconds while teacher captions continued. These are measured synthetic-input timings, not guarantees. Physical microphone, notch animation and the finished video still need rehearsal.

## Shutdown regression

The October 11 shutdown fix schedules signal-triggered termination on the run loop, outside the main dispatch callback. This lets the asynchronous board save run before quitting. The empty native regression app verifies SIGTERM, SIGINT, and a declined save followed by a successful retry; it opens no microphone or model session. An older already-running build can remain stuck in its previous quit callback. Do not force-quit it until any unsaved work has been accounted for.
